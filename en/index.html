macOS app

Quileg HID

Let AI actually use your iPhone

Screen mirroring gives the AI eyes; an ESP32-S3 board poses as a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse to do the tapping. Taps, long presses, typing, scrolling — and every step is confirmed by looking at the screen again.

  • macOS 13+ · Apple silicon / Intel
  • Needs an ESP32-S3 board (around US$4–9)
  • Control service binds to 127.0.0.1:8890 only

iPhone · iPad · Mac

Guyun

Your files, in your own bucket

Connect your own object storage — Alibaba Cloud OSS, Tencent COS, Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, MinIO, or any S3-compatible service. No accounts, and the keys stay in this device's keychain.

  • iOS 17 / iPadOS 17 / macOS 14 and later
  • App is free; storage is billed by your provider
  • No relay server — files go straight to your bucket

Quileg STM32

Let the computer do the tapping

A small device that presents itself as a standard USB keyboard and mouse: you compose the routine in a web page, it travels over serial to the board, and the firmware turns it into real key presses and pointer movement. No API on the target, no client on the computer. Firmware, web host and serial protocol for the first two generations, released under MIT as they were.

  • MIT licence · STM32F103C8T6
  • Archived — no support, kept for learning and rebuilding
  • Generation three is Quileg HID: Bluetooth, and the target is the phone
Visit the archive → Source

Two products, one approach

One less server layer, wherever it is possible.

  1. 01

    Local first

    Quileg HID's control service listens on 127.0.0.1 only, so the picture and the actions never leave your Mac; Guyun has no relay server — requests go from your device straight to your own bucket.

  2. 02

    Hands off your data

    Guyun has no accounts, no servers of ours and no telemetry, and the keys stay in this device's keychain. Quileg HID processes the screen and the actions locally.

  3. 03

    Free to use

    Only two things cost money, and both stay in your hands: an ESP32-S3 board (around US$4–9), or the storage bill for your own bucket.

About

This is where I keep the two tools I build. They solve the same kind of problem: keeping work that would otherwise pass through someone else's server on your own devices.

If something misbehaves or you want a feature, a GitHub issue is the most direct route — both repositories are open.
